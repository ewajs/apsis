# 0014. AR: the phone's own viewers

- Status: Accepted (taken unsupervised, 2026-11-28). A spacecraft in AR from step 6.1;
  the tabletop solar system in 6.2, live in WebXR on Android (6.3). Amended 2026-11-28
  (v0.7.1): two sizes, and Quick Look's files built ahead.
- Date: 2026-11-28

## Decision

- **Hand off, don't build:** a spacecraft opens in the phone's own AR viewer, Scene Viewer
  on Android and Quick Look on iPhones and iPads (ADR 0001). They place, light, scale and
  photograph better than anything we'd write, and cost us no code in the bundle.
- **The AR copy:** `npm run models` writes `public/models/ar/<id>.glb` beside each model:
  plain glTF (no Meshopt, no quantisation, no WebP: Scene Viewer's extension support isn't
  documented, plain is safe), textures at most 1024 px and JPEG unless see-through,
  **standing up** the way both viewers place a model on the floor: the antenna (+Z) up,
  its lowest point at floor level, centred. Real size. A copy over 5 MB is simplified,
  then its textures halved. Europa Clipper stays at 12.5 MB: its mesh is mostly loose
  triangles that the simplifier can't merge.
- **Two sizes** (v0.7.1; the owner's call, after real size proved too big for a room):
  "En tu casa (RA)", **1 m along its longest side**, which the viewer lets you resize,
  and "Tamaño real (RA)", locked so it stays true. The card says what to expect under
  each, "1 m · escala 1:109" and "109 × 57 m, 67 m de alto"; Scene Viewer shows the
  same in its title. The 1 m copy is the same file under one scaling node: the models
  aren't touched.
- **Android:** each size is a plain link, an `intent://` URL that opens Scene Viewer
  (`core/ar.ts`) with the file's absolute URL, `ar_preferred`, `resizable` true for the
  1 m copy and false for the real size, falling back to the page.
- **iPhone:** Quick Look reads USDZ, not glTF. Its files are **built ahead** (v0.7.1),
  `ar/<id>.usdz` and `ar/<id>-1m.usdz`, and opened straight from the tap through a link
  with `rel="ar"` around an image; the real size adds `#allowsContentScaling=0`. Before,
  the page converted the AR copy on every tap, which took seconds for a large model,
  and that's gone: offline, a file not seen before doesn't open.
- **How the USDZ files are made** (`scripts/models/ar-variants.ts`): three's
  `USDZExporter`, the converter the page used, run in headless Chromium (it needs a
  canvas for textures). It writes USD as text, three times the GLB (Europa Clipper:
  37.6 MB); Pixar's usd-core rewrites it in binary and packages it for ARKit
  (`usdz_binary.py`, Python, a pinned virtual environment under `.cache/` made on first
  use), back to the GLB's size (11.3 MB) with the same meshes, points and textures. Only
  `npm run models` needs it; CI and the app don't.
- **Which viewer:** Safari announces Quick Look (`relList.supports('ar')`); Android is
  recognised by its user agent; elsewhere the card offers no AR. `?ar=` forces one, for
  tests.
- **The tabletop solar system (6.2)** is made at the shown instant, so it only exists in
  the page: Quick Look takes it as a blob, but Scene Viewer, another app, can only
  fetch URLs. On Android it's live WebXR in the page instead (6.2–6.3): hit-test to find
  a surface, a tap to place it. Laid out by `core/tabletop.ts`: the map's log scale for
  distances (60 cm to 200 AU), log-enlarged sizes, real angles above the ecliptic.

## Alternatives

- **`<model-viewer>`:** does all of this and WebXR, but bundles a second copy of three.js
  (hundreds of KB) for two links and a converter we already have.
- **USDZ converted in the page** (6.1 to 7.0): no extra files, but seconds of work on
  every tap for a large model, and Quick Look's options need a real URL.
- **Text USDZ, as three writes it:** no Python step, but three times the download.
- **A lighter 1 m copy** (fewer triangles, smaller textures, for a faster tap): the owner
  chose the full models at both sizes.
